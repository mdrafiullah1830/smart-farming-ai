# Somapura Mahavihara

## Overview
Somapura Mahavihara in Paharpur, Naogaon (Rajshahi Division) is the largest Buddhist monastery south of the Himalayas and a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1985). Built in the 8th century by Dharmapala of the Pala dynasty, it was a renowned intellectual center for 300 years.

## History
- **Founded**: Late 8th century (c. 781-821 CE) by King Dharmapala (Pala Empire)
- **Peak**: 9th-12th centuries, international center of learning
- **Scholars**: Atisha Dipankara (11th century) studied here before going to Tibet
- **Decline**: 12th century (Sena dynasty, Muslim invasions)
- **Rediscovery**: 1807 by Buchanan Hamilton; excavated 1920s-1990s
- **UNESCO inscription**: 1985 (criteria i, ii, vi)

## Architecture
- **Plan**: Quadrangular, 274m × 274m (18 acres)
- **Main shrine**: Central cruciform temple, 21m high (originally 30m+)
- **Monastic cells**: 177 cells around courtyard (45 per side + corners)
- **Entrance**: Elaborate northern gateway with guard rooms
- **Materials**: Terracotta bricks, baked bricks, mud mortar
- **Terracotta plaques**: ~2,800 plaques depicting Buddhist, Hindu, daily life scenes

## Key Features
1. **Central Temple** — Cruciform plan, three terraces, circumambulatory path, stucco sculptures
2. **Monastic Cells** — Each 4.3m × 4.3m, niches for lamps/images, some with brick beds
3. **Terracotta Art** — Buddha life scenes, Jataka tales, Hindu deities, musicians, dancers, animals
4. **Stupas & Shrines** — Votive stupas, subsidiary shrines in courtyard
5. **Drainage System** — Sophisticated covered drains, inspection chambers
6. **Water Management** — Wells, reservoirs, bath complex

## Museum (Site Museum)
- **Established**: 1957, expanded 1990s
- **Collection**: Terracotta plaques, bronze Buddha images, stone sculptures, coins, inscriptions, pottery, seals
- **Highlights**: Bronze Avalokiteshvara, stone Buddha heads, inscribed terracotta seal

## Visiting Information
- **Location**: Paharpur, Badalgachhi Upazila, Naogaon District
- **Hours**: Saturday-Wednesday 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (summer), 9:00 AM - 4:30 PM (winter); Friday 2:30 PM - 5:00 PM/4:30 PM
- **Closed**: Thursday, government holidays
- **Entry Fee**: Bangladeshi ৳20, SAARC ৳100, Foreign ৳200
- **Photography**: Allowed (no tripod without permission)

## How to Reach
- **From Dhaka**: 5-6 hours by road (250 km via Jamuna Bridge)
- **From Rajshahi**: 1.5 hours (70 km)
- **Nearest town**: Badalgachhi (5 km), Naogaon (30 km)
- **Public transport**: Bus to Naogaon/Joypurhat, then CNG/auto to Paharpur
- **Railway**: Nearest station - Jamalganj (15 km) or Santahar (40 km)

## Nearby Attractions
- **Kusumba Mosque** (25 km) — 1558 stone mosque
- **Jagaddal Mahavihara** (15 km) — Smaller monastery ruins
- **Halud Vihara** (10 km) — Another monastery site
- **Naogaon town** — Local market, food

## Cultural Significance
- **Buddhist cosmology** — Mandala layout represents Mount Meru
- **Pala art** — Masterpiece of Pala-Sena sculpture/architecture
- **International connections** — Tibet, Myanmar, Southeast Asia, China
- **Educational legacy** — Model for Nalanda, Vikramashila, Tibetan monasteries

## Best Time to Visit
October-March (cool, dry). October-November for clear skies and photography. Avoid April-June (extreme heat).

## Tips
- Hire guide from museum for detailed interpretation
- Morning light best for terracotta photography
- Carry water, hat, sunscreen (limited shade)
- Combine with Kusumba Mosque day trip from Rajshahi
- Respect site — no climbing on ruins
- Museum closes 30 min before site
